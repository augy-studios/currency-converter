Privacy

Last updated 16 November 2026

uwuCurrency (Currency Converter by UwU Apps) is made by Augy Studios. This page says what uwuCurrency keeps about you, where it goes, and how to delete it. It covers the website at currency.uwuapps.org, the Android app (from Google Play or GitHub), and the Windows, macOS and Linux apps.

The short version

  • There are no accounts. You don't sign up, and we don't know who you are.
  • Your conversion history and settings stay on your device. Nothing is sent to us.
  • The apps have no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and don't use your device's advertising ID. The website has Google Analytics and Google ads (section 5).
  • Nothing about you is sold.

1. What stays on your device

uwuCurrency keeps these on your device only, in your browser's storage on the website and in the app's own storage in the apps:

  • the amount and currencies you last converted, so they're there next time;
  • your conversion history: each conversion's amount, currencies, rate, result and when you made it, up to the last 500;
  • your theme (light, dark or time-based, and the colour), and which tours you've seen;
  • the last exchange rates fetched, so the converter works offline.

None of this is sent to uwuCurrency's server or anyone else, except when you choose to share it with another of your devices (section 3).

2. Exchange rates

To convert, uwuCurrency asks its server for exchange rates: which currency you're converting from, and for the graph, which currencies and dates. Not the amount. The server gets them from Augy Studios' own exchange rate service (api.currency.uwuapps.org, built on Frankfurter), which sees the server's address, not yours.

3. Sharing history with another device

Share with another device copies conversions and your theme straight from one of your devices to another, with a code or QR code. To connect the two devices:

  • A free public service, PeerJS (0.peerjs.com), introduces them. It sees both devices' IP addresses and a random code, but not your history.
  • Google's and Cloudflare's STUN servers tell each device its own public address. They see your IP address.

Your conversions then go straight from one device to the other, encrypted on the way (WebRTC with DTLS). They don't pass through uwuCurrency's server, and the other device learns your device's IP address.

4. Report a bug

If you use Report a bug (in the edge tray), what you send goes to uwuCurrency's server and on to Augy Studios as a message from a Telegram bot. It contains:

  • what you typed, and a way to reach you, if you give one;
  • up to three pictures, if you add them: from your device, or a screenshot of a page of the app. A screenshot shows whatever is on that page, which can include your history;
  • what helps find the bug, all shown to you before it's sent: the app's version, which app (website, Android, desktop), your device and system as your browser or app describes them, and the kind of page you were on. Never the amounts or currencies you typed, unless a picture you add shows them.

uwuCurrency's server doesn't store reports. They're kept in Augy Studios' Telegram chats, which Telegram stores under its own privacy policy. To limit misuse, the server counts reports from each IP address, for a minute and, where set up, for an hour; then the count is gone. Each report gets an ID (like 20261116-7K3Q): quote it to have that report deleted.

5. The website

  • Google Analytics counts visits to the website's pages: which pages are opened, roughly where from, and on what kind of device and browser. It uses cookies to tell visits apart. It's on the website only, not in the apps.
  • Google AdSense shows ads on the website, and Google may use cookies to choose them. You can change how Google personalises ads at adssettings.google.com. There are no ads in the apps.
  • Google Fonts serves the website's font, and jsDelivr its graph and picture libraries, so they see your IP address when they load. The apps carry their own.
  • For sharing history, the website loads the PeerJS library from cdnjs (Cloudflare).
  • Buy Augy a Coffee opens Stripe's page, only if you click it. What you give there is handled by Stripe.

Your browser's settings or an ad blocker can stop the analytics and ads; the converter works the same without them.

6. Updates and notifications

  • The Android app from GitHub asks uwuCurrency's server whether there's a new version, and downloads it from GitHub, which sees your IP address.
  • "New versions of the app" (Android, off unless you turn it on) uses Firebase Cloud Messaging, Google's push service. Your phone joins a topic for uwuCurrency's new versions, and Google handles the address it sends notifications to. uwuCurrency's server keeps no list of phones.
  • The Android app from Google Play is updated by Google Play, and has no updater of its own.
  • The Windows, macOS and Linux apps check GitHub for new versions themselves, about once an hour.

7. Android permissions

  • Internet: for exchange rates, sharing history and Report a bug.
  • Notifications: only asked for when you turn on "New versions of the app".
  • Install apps: the GitHub version only, to install its own updates when you choose Update. The Google Play version doesn't have it.
  • Keep the screen on: only while sharing with another device, so the connection isn't dropped.

uwuCurrency doesn't use your location, contacts, camera, microphone or advertising ID.

9. Deleting your data

  • Your history: Clear on the History card deletes every conversion on that device; the × beside one deletes just that one. Other devices keep their own copies until you clear them there.
  • Everything on a device: clear the website's data in your browser, or uninstall the app.
  • Bug reports: email us and quote the report's ID.

10. Your choices and rights

You can use uwuCurrency without giving any personal details at all. Depending on where you live (for example under the UK or EU GDPR, or Singapore's PDPA), you may have the right to see the data we hold about you, have it corrected or deleted, take a copy, or object to how it's used. As we hold nothing but bug reports you send, email us about those, and we'll answer within 30 days. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

11. Keeping it safe

Everything between the apps and uwuCurrency's server is sent over HTTPS, and sharing with another device is encrypted from device to device. No service is perfectly secure, but if something goes wrong that affects your data, we'll tell you.

12. Children

uwuCurrency is suitable for everyone, and collects no personal data from anyone, children included. The website's ads come from Google; the apps have none.

13. Changes to this page

If what uwuCurrency does with your data changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top says when. Bigger changes are also said in the apps' release notes.
